# Legend — six-perspective analysis (2026-07-21)

A team of six persona-analysts reviewed Legend "as a whole," each grounded in the
actual repository (files read, `file:line` cited, gate built and run) before
opining. Each was told to be a critical friend, not a cheerleader. This document
is the synthesis followed by all six memos verbatim.

- **Dr. Elena Vásquez** — cognitive neuroscience / memory science
- **Marcus Chen** — product / founder
- **Raymond Okafor** — master systems programmer (built & ran the gate)
- **Dr. Priya Nair** — ML / LLM-memory research & eval rigor
- **Dana Kroll** — skeptic / red-team
- **Sofia Almeida** — developer-experience / knowledge-representation

State at time of review: branch `v3_minimal`; shipping artifact is the single-file
C oracle `legend.c` (~10.3k lines) + `embed.c` (BGE-small); live longitudinal
trial in `~/Code/alchamancer2` on Round 5.

---

## Synthesis

### The meta-finding

**The craftsmanship has outrun the validation.** Ray built and ran the whole
thing and calls it production-grade C — gate all-green in 57s, a persistence
reader that's "the best code in the file," transactional writes for free from the
plan/apply split. And every other lens, independently, says the *value of the
defining feature* — a deduplicated causal graph over a plain notes file or naive
RAG — is **unproven, and the one experiment designed to prove it has never been
run.** Those two facts don't contradict; together they *are* the diagnosis. An
excellent answer built, the question not yet shown to need it.

### Where all six converge

1. **The evidence is structurally self-referential** (Priya, Dana, Marcus;
   corroborated by Elena). Author = subject = grader = Claude; the only
   quantitative lift (conflictqa 8/8) has *no baseline isolating the graph* (66
   facts fit in-context → store-vs-nothing on 8 synthetic questions); the one
   rigorous eval (simpleqa §1-12, token-parity RAG arm + broken-flip tracking) is
   **designed but unrun**, its `flips.md` verification boxes unchecked. Elena's
   version is sharpest: the schema-override effect (model's prior beating a
   correct store; multi-hop capped 2/4) **appeared in your own conflictqa data and
   was engineered away** (synthetic-entity pivot) rather than characterized. "It
   is not a confound. It is the phenomenon."

2. **Discipline belongs in the substrate, not the prose.** Sofia's root cause:
   two missing distinctions — no literal type (every string reifies to a node) and
   mint-on-unknown-reference (no `must_exist` inverse to `forced_new`). The trial
   ran the experiment — 400-char rule, 98% violation, "the instruction is dead" —
   and the fix that shipped for R4's worst papercut was *another instruction
   line*. Marcus: "leaky core." Dana: structural. The model respects hard errors
   (zero rejections) and ignores soft ones.

3. **The causal layer is the flagship that isn't earning its keep.** Priya credits
   causal-rung + modal edge typing as the one genuinely novel primitive — but its
   usefulness is unproven; `correlated_with` is 0 across 39 causal facts. Elena:
   "never promote correlation to cause" guards an internal op nobody performs
   while the real threat (ingesting LLM stamping `caused` on a correlation) is
   unguarded; `derived_from` is provenance dressed as a Pearl SCM. Marcus & Dana:
   don't lead with it.

### Where they genuinely disagree (the useful part)

- **Ship now vs. prove first.** Marcus wants to ship the OSS "memory for coding
  agents" wedge this week and learn from a real external user; Dana wants to
  freeze the surface and starve the trial until the RAG comparison runs. Both
  agree the causal graph shouldn't be the headline. They answer *different*
  questions (does anyone but you get value / does the graph beat RAG); neither
  costs code — do both.

- **Is the neuroscience load-bearing?** Elena grades the science ~⅔ load-bearing
  and would back the team; Dana calls the brain framing "cosplay." Narrower than
  it looks: Elena's load-bearing third is *architecture decisions* (no-raw-storage,
  prediction-error-gated supersession), and on the *causal layer* she agrees it's
  ornamental. Her prescription: keep the architecture, demote the neuroscience
  from mechanism to scaffolding in the prose, run the two ablations.

- **Novelty vs. moat.** Priya: causal-rung edges are genuinely novel. Marcus:
  no moat — Zep and mem0 already own the ground with companies attached. Both
  true: novel ≠ defensible.

### Prioritized actions (attributed)

1. **Run the experiment you already designed.** simpleqa §1-12 at ~500 questions,
   arm B (Legend) vs arm D (naive RAG) at token-budget parity, external/blind
   grader, pre-register the structure delta (B−D) as the primary endpoint with the
   CI. (Priya #6, Dana's gate, Marcus's "answer why-not-MEMORY.md," Elena's
   ablations bolt on.) Highest-value move; execution, not conception. Cheaper
   parallel first cut: Marcus's one external user, two weeks.
2. **Move the write contract into the substrate** (Sofia): literals stored on
   relations in value positions; reference positions (`changes.target`,
   `resolves.o`, merge operands) must resolve or error with near-miss candidates.
   Retires 5 of 7 audit checks and the papercut treadmill; the plan/apply seam
   Ray praised is where the validation belongs.
3. **Reframe the story** (Marcus + Elena): bury the causal/biological framing as
   internals; lead with the orientation packet + one-command install; demote
   neuroscience to scaffolding in the docs and fix Elena's three concrete errors
   (pattern-separation mislabel, provenance≠SCM, the correlation-guard).
4. **Cheap engineering hygiene** (Ray): fix the embedder identity drift (comments
   say MiniLM/mean-pool/6-layer, code is BGE/CLS/12-layer, weights named
   `minilm.int8.bin`) — an afternoon that retires a silent-corruption path; one
   comment on the non-reentrant `apply_plan` scratch tripwire; drop the 133MB
   safetensors from git.

### One correction to the record

The store is **not** LZ4 + MessagePack (that was the Rust v1/v2 design). The C
oracle uses a hand-rolled length-prefixed little-endian binary format
(`legend.c:7856` `snapshot_serialize`) — self-describing and self-validating.
`grep -i lz4` returns nothing.

# Memo 1 — Marcus Chen (product)

**Re:** Is "long-term memory for LLMs" a product, and would I put money behind it

## Thesis (one line)
Legend is a beautifully-engineered answer to a real and growing pain — agents
forget between sessions — but today it's a research artifact with an n=1 dogfood
trial, and its single most valuable use case is already being won by a free
markdown file the same agent writes to. Fund the wedge, not the graph.

## 1. Who is the user, what job, and does the ICP ambiguity hurt

The positioning is "long-term memory for LLMs — **including you**" (README.md:3-7;
new_foundation_v0_core.md:84). That "including you" is the whole problem in three
words. Legend can't decide if the customer is the agent or the human.

Look at who actually touches the surface in the trial:
- The **agent** calls `save`/`recall`/`audit` over MCP. It is the literal API
  consumer.
- But every unit of *value realization and quality control* is a **human** action:
  Nick runs the trial, files "legend trial issue" elements in-band
  (alchamancer-trial.md:118-122), asks the audit question "did you use legend
  after that last prompt?" (alchamancer-trial.md:351-354) — which the doc
  explicitly calls "the only instrument that catches missing saves" — and runs the
  human-in-the-loop `maintain` gardening pass (alchamancer-trial.md:759-765).

So the pattern is: the agent is the *user*, the human is the *operator and
janitor*. That's a coherent model — but the product hasn't admitted it. The
job-to-be-hired is best stated from the human's chair: **"stop my coding agent
from re-deriving decisions and re-making mistakes every session."** The verdict
data agrees — the wins that landed were decisions, deferred levers, and procedural
recipes carried across sessions (alchamancer-trial.md:513-524).

The ambiguity hurts because it splits the roadmap. Every "instruction nudge" fix
(findings 1-10) is an attempt to make the *agent* a disciplined user; every
audit/maintain feature is an attempt to give the *human* a mop. You're building
two products to serve one loop. Pick the human as the buyer and the agent as the
runtime, and the roadmap collapses to one thing.

## 2. The wedge — feature or product?

"Revisable, deduplicated, causal reality graph as MCP memory" is **four features
in a trench coat**, and the trial tells you which ones the market actually uses:

- **Revisable / supersession (`changes`, retract, merge):** used and valued. This
  is real.
- **Dedup / canonical names:** used, but only because a human and an endless
  instruction treadmill keep it clean.
- **Causal predicates + Pearl rungs + modality:** *barely used.* Across the trial:
  `modal` fired 5 times, `correlated_with` **0 times across 39 causal facts**
  (alchamancer-trial.md:855-856, 862-869). The single most novel, differentiated
  piece of engineering is dead weight in the field.

The honest wedge is narrower and less sexy than the pitch: **a cross-session,
revisable memory an agent writes structured facts into and gets a focused
orientation packet out of at session start.** The orientation packet is the actual
hero — it's cited as load-bearing in nearly every positive testimony
(alchamancer-trial.md:198-204, 280-290, 509-524).

That's a product. The causal reality graph is a research bet stapled to it. Don't
lead with the bet.

## 3. Competitive landscape — where Legend wins, ties, loses

| Competitor | What it is | Legend vs it |
|---|---|---|
| **Long-context (Gemini 1-2M, Claude 1M)** | Brute-force recall in-window | Legend **wins** on persistence + cost across sessions; loses on zero-setup |
| **RAG / vector stores (Pinecone, pgvector, Chroma)** | Chunk retrieval | Legend **wins** on revision/supersession — RAG is append-only and rots; ties on retrieval quality (and Legend's own trial says retrieval is its *weakest* surface) |
| **mem0** | OSS memory layer, LLM extraction + consolidation, graph option, huge mindshare | Legend **loses on distribution/mindshare**, ties on capability. mem0 is the category's default noun. |
| **Zep / Graphiti** | Temporal knowledge graph, bi-temporal, **fact invalidation**, entity resolution, hosted + OSS | This is Legend's **twin**. Zep already ships supersession + temporal graph + a company behind it. Legend **loses on being a funded product**, arguably ties on design taste. |
| **Letta / MemGPT** | Agent runtime with self-editing tiered memory | Different altitude — Letta owns the whole agent. Legend **loses** if the buyer wants a framework, **wins** if they want a memory primitive under their own agent. |
| **LangMem / LangGraph memory** | Framework-coupled memory (semantic/episodic/procedural) | Legend **wins** on being framework-agnostic (plain MCP + C binary); loses to LangChain's gravity. |
| **Native memory (Anthropic memory tool, OpenAI/ChatGPT memory)** | Provider-owned, auto-injected files/memory | **The existential one.** See below. |

**The moat question, blunt: there isn't one.** Not a data moat (every store is
local, private, single-tenant — no aggregation, no network effect). Not a
switching-cost moat (a store is small and journal-reconstructable). Not a
distribution moat (zero users but the author). The genuine differentiators —
single-file C99, zero runtime deps, embed-in-binary, local-first, MCP-native
self-provisioning (`legend init` writes its own `.mcp.json`, mcp-server.md:41-54)
— are **taste and portability, not defensibility.** Anyone can rebuild a
single-file graph in a weekend once the idea is proven. Zep and mem0 already did
the hard version with a company attached.

## 4. Is alchamancer2 real PMF signal?

No. It's high-quality **dogfooding**, which is valuable for product development and
worthless as market validation. Be clear-eyed about the confound: the founder
built the tool, runs the tool, on the founder's own host, and the founder
**grades the tool** — the GO verdict (alchamancer-trial.md:509-531) is Legend's
author self-reporting that Legend earned its keep. Every testimony is the same
agent lineage in the same repo. n=1, single-host, self-scored. That's not a signal
you can raise on or bet a roadmap on.

Worse, the trial keeps surfacing its own null result and not naming it: procedural
recipes and debugging knowledge — *exactly* the highest-value category —
repeatedly went to Claude's **own `.claude/MEMORY.md` file memory, not Legend**
(alchamancer-trial.md:206-218, 264-267, 691-693). Finding 8 calls it "two
competing memory surfaces." I'd call it the market speaking: given a free,
zero-ceremony, auto-injected markdown file versus a disciplined structured graph,
the agent picks the markdown file for most knowledge. That's a competitive loss
recorded inside your own PMF evidence.

**Cheapest honest experiment (do this before anything else):** one external
developer, their own repo, two weeks, the same journal + testimony ritual you
already have. It costs you a `legend init`, a Loom, and a follow-up call. It
answers the only question that matters: *does the value survive contact with
someone who didn't build it?* Everything else — the SimpleQA distillation study
(eval-simpleqa-distillation.md, still DRAFT/Phase-0, gated on an OpenAI key and
unverified model IDs) — is a research paper, not a customer. Do not spend
$125-200 on a benchmark before you've spent $0 on a second human.

Second-cheapest, and higher-signal: the Claude-vs-Codex axis the doc keeps
deferring (alchamancer-trial.md:506-507) — but only counts as external if it's
someone else's project.

## 5. Packaging & GTM — what to ship first, to whom

Today Legend is a **research artifact wearing an MCP-server costume**. The good
news: the costume is the right one.

Ship it as an **open-source, local-first MCP memory server for coding agents.**
Concretely:
- **Package:** the binary + `legend init` self-provisioning is already a
  one-command install (mcp-server.md:53-54). That's your entire onboarding. Lean
  all the way into it.
- **Buyer/beachhead:** developers running Claude Code / Cursor / Codex who feel
  "my agent re-explains the codebase every session." That pain is acute, the trial
  evidence is strongest there (the AP-migration session collapsed a git-history
  diagnosis to one minute, alchamancer-trial.md:674-676), and MCP is the native
  install path.
- **Sequence:** OSS primitive first (win developer trust + GitHub distribution,
  the thing you're most behind on vs mem0/Zep) → *maybe* a hosted/team version
  later if multi-agent shared stores show demand. Do **not** start with the hosted
  service or the distillation-artifact story; both are ahead of your evidence.

What NOT to ship as the headline: the causal graph, the Pearl rungs, the
biological-region architecture. They're the reason an engineer will love the
codebase and the reason a customer will bounce off the pitch. Bury them as
internals.

## 6. The write-discipline failures — killer, UX, or noise?

Neither killer nor noise. It's a **UX symptom of the central architectural bet,
and the symptom is telling you the bet is leaking.**

The pattern: Legend offloads all quality discipline (recall-before-save,
`changes`-not-facts, canonical-names-not-prose, summaries-under-400) onto the
calling LLM via prose instructions. Then it spends the entire trial discovering
the LLM ignores the soft ones. The 400-char rule is the clean proof: shipped
explicitly, then **43 of 44 summaries blew past it — 98% violation vs 74%
historical — and the doc's own words are "the write-side length instruction is
dead"** (alchamancer-trial.md:838-848). The predicate vocabulary fragmented 19→84
(alchamancer-trial.md:413). Prose keeps getting reified into element names via
three different paths (findings 6, 10; `#615`; the `changes.to` path,
alchamancer-trial.md:851-856).

Why it's not a killer: the store still works, and `audit`/`maintain`
(cli.md:63-99) is a real human backstop that catches the wreckage.

Why it's not noise: it means **store quality degrades silently with use and
requires periodic human gardening to stay useful.** A memory product whose
fidelity depends on an LLM obeying prose rules it demonstrably won't obey has a
leaky core, and the fix-treadmill (findings 1-10, an 11-item watch list) is
unbounded — every fix is another instruction line the next model may ignore. The
right lesson, which the trial half-learned, is that discipline belongs in the
*substrate* (validate/normalize on write) or in the *deterministic audit*, not in
prose aimed at the LLM. Until that's internalized, every new capability ships with
a new class of silent corruption.

## The single biggest product risk

Not the write discipline. **It's that Legend's best-proven value competes against
a free file the same agent already writes to and gets auto-injected —
`.claude/MEMORY.md` / the provider-native memory tool.** Your own trial documents
the agent choosing that file over Legend for the highest-value knowledge,
repeatedly (alchamancer-trial.md:206-218). Provider-native memory (Anthropic's
memory tool, ChatGPT memory) is the platform absorbing this layer from above. If
the differentiated value (structured revision, supersession, cross-tool sharing)
can't be made *more convenient* than "just append to a markdown file the model
already reads," Legend loses to zero-friction — and convenience, not capability,
is what the trial shows deciding the outcome.

## Bottom line — would I fund/ship it, and in what form

**As a company raising on this evidence: no.** n=1 self-graded dogfood, no moat,
and two funded competitors (Zep, mem0) already own the conceptual and distribution
ground. I would not write a check on the trial as it stands.

**As a project worth continuing: yes — but reframed and de-risked in this order:**
1. **Get one external user this week** (the $0 experiment). Nothing else is real
   until value survives someone who didn't build it.
2. **Ship the OSS MCP memory-for-coding-agents wedge.** Bury the causal graph. Win
   on the orientation packet + one-command install.
3. **Move discipline out of prose and into the substrate.** Stop patching the
   model; make bad writes structurally hard.
4. **Directly answer "why not just use MEMORY.md?"** — in the README, in the demo,
   in the architecture. If there's no crisp answer, that's your real go/no-go, not
   the SimpleQA study.

The engineering here is genuinely excellent and the pain is real and growing. But
right now this is a superb solution in search of its second user, carrying
research bets the market isn't paying for. Fund the wedge and the external
validation; shelve the graph philosophy until a customer asks for it.

# Memo 2 — Dana Kroll (skeptic / red-team)

**Re:** Is this worth continuing? **Posture:** Adversarial. Every attack is
anchored to something I read.

## Thesis

Legend is a genuinely well-engineered answer to a question nobody has yet shown
needs asking. The craftsmanship is real — the determinism discipline in particular
is better than most funded projects. But after ~431 commits, three ground-up
architectures, and a two-week live trial, **the project has not produced a single
piece of evidence that its defining feature — a deduplicated, causal, revisable
*graph* — beats the two cheap alternatives it must beat: a well-structured
markdown file, and naive RAG.** The one eval designed to test exactly that
comparison sits unrun in the repo. Everything positive on the board is either a
synthetic tautology or the author's own agent agreeing with the author's own
agent. My recommendation is **conditional continue → hard gate**: do not deepen,
do not add surface, until it clears one falsifiable bar it wrote for itself and
has been avoiding.

Fair up front, because a red-teamer who only attacks gets ignored:

- **The determinism engineering is excellent.** Build-aware byte-identical journal
  replay across 11 binary builds (`docs/alchamancer-trial.md:69-74`) is a real,
  honest, reproducible integrity guarantee. Most teams can't do this.
- **The trial is logged with unusual honesty.** It records self-inflicted store
  corruption (`alchamancer-trial.md:233-251`), negative results, and — critically
  — an explicit "don't credit Legend for wins that came from the file-based
  memory" rule (`alchamancer-trial.md:691-693`). That intellectual honesty is why
  I can attack this at all.
- **One genuinely graph-native insight exists:** stale recorded intent surfaced a
  real code bug because the memory *disagreed* with the code
  (`alchamancer-trial.md:678-682`, `#89`). That's the single most interesting
  argument in the whole corpus for why structured memory ≠ a notes file. I'll come
  back to how thin it still is.

## The 5 strongest attacks, ranked by lethality

### 1. The core value proposition has never been tested against the cheaper alternative — and where it *was* tested honestly, the graph lost. (LETHAL)

The whole premise is that a deduped causal graph buys something over "write a good
markdown file" or "throw the corpus in a vector store." The project *knows* this
is the question — it wrote the test. `docs/eval-simpleqa-distillation.md:54` names
the exact bar: *"Structure — the deduped graph earns something over dumb
retrieval,"* measured as arm B (Legend) − arm D (naive RAG) over an identical,
token-budget-matched corpus. And `:57`: *"If RAG matches Legend, the structure
isn't paying for itself on this task shape."*

That study is `Status: DRAFT` (`:3`), has never been run, requires an API key that
isn't present, and is specced against **GPT-5.6 "released 2026-07-09"** (`:120`) —
a model that maps to nothing you can actually call. The one experiment that would
validate the thesis is vaporware.

Meanwhile, the eval that *was* run tells the damning version. In `conflictqa`, on
**real (famous) entities**, multi-hop retrieval capped at **2/4 even with a
correct store**, because the consuming model overrode the retrieved facts
(`memory/project_conflictqa_eval.md`). The clean **8/8** headline comes only from
the *synthetic* variant — alien-name entities engineered so the model has zero
parametric prior and "defers to Legend completely." That's not a memory benchmark;
it's a check that retrieval returns the row you inserted, with N=8. The store
demonstrably wins only in the one regime where every alternative would also win
(the model knows nothing) and demonstrably *fails to overcome the consumer* in the
regime that matters (the model has priors). Net measured edge of the graph over a
notes file or RAG: **zero.**

### 2. The trial measures the author's belief in the tool, not the tool. (LETHAL)

Enumerate the confounds without mercy:
- **N=1 user**, who is the tool's author.
- **Host = the author's own game project** (`alchamancer2`).
- **The agent being helped and the agent judging "was this useful?" are both
  Claude.** The evaluation instrument is literally Claude rating its own recall out
  of 10 ("recall self-rated ~3/10", `alchamancer-trial.md:198`).
- **The verdict was rendered by the same agent lineage that built the thing** —
  "VERDICT RENDERED 2026-07-15 — GO" (`alchamancer-trial.md:509`), off three
  countdown sessions.
- **Rounds close early and say so:** Round 4 "CLOSED — 7 sessions (below the 15–18
  target; findings directional)" (`alchamancer-trial.md:838`).

And the trial's own record keeps handing the win to the free alternative: the most
reusable lessons — the Sora motion rule, the OOM-segfault recipe
(`alchamancer-trial.md:266-267`) — went to the built-in `.claude/MEMORY.md`,
**not** Legend. Finding 8 is an entire benchmark-work session where "NONE" went to
Legend (`alchamancer-trial.md:206-218`). That is attack #1's cheaper alternative
beating Legend *inside the author's own trial*, recorded in Legend's own trial
log. A trial that can't beat the markdown file it ships next to, refereed by the
system that authored it, proves belief, not value.

### 3. The memory system cannot make the model obey its own writing rules — after five rounds. (SEVERE)

This is the most damning *internal* signal, because it's the product failing at
its one job. A deduped, canonical, causal graph is only worth more than prose if
the writes are clean. They are not, and the trial pre-registered the gauge and
watched it fail:

- Round 4, gauge 2: **43 of 44 summaries exceeded the 400-char limit (98%, vs 74%
  historically); "The write-side length instruction is dead (`#150`)"**
  (`alchamancer-trial.md:846-848`).
- `changes.to` reified prose **4 of 5 times, minting 300–542-char element names**
  (`alchamancer-trial.md:850-852`).
- The model **minted off-list kinds** — `reference` (11×), `feedback` (2×):
  "taxonomy drift, directional" (`alchamancer-trial.md:859-860`).
- Predicate vocabulary fragmented **19 → 84**, long single-use tail, including a
  `current_current_status` doubled-prefix bug (`alchamancer-trial.md:413-416`).

The response every round is "another instruction line," and the next round shows
it didn't land. This isn't a papercut; it's structural. You are using a
non-deterministic writer to maintain a substrate whose entire value depends on
determinism and canonicalization. The graph's cleanliness is the product, and the
product cannot enforce it.

### 4. This is v3 of a thrash, and the "source of truth" spec describes code that does not exist. (SERIOUS)

`CLAUDE.md:10` names `new_foundation_v0_core.md` as "v0 scope (build this first)."
That document specifies a **Rust** system: an in-house pure-Rust BERT engine in
`src/inference/`, GLiNER1 NER, `tantivy`, MiniLM baked into the binary, a 20-week
build order (`:1666`), a notes-app reference frontend at step 13 (`:1660`).
**None of it exists.** There is no `src/`, no `Cargo.toml`. The actual artifact is
`legend.c` — 10,304 lines of C — plus `embed.c` (658), using BGE-small. The
canonical build spec describes an abandoned architecture.

That's the churn made concrete, and the memory index is its graveyard
(`memory/MEMORY.md:70-84`): GLiNER2 removed, notes-app dropped, MiniLM→BGE, daemon
removed, counting in-scope→out-of-scope→back-in, MemoryAgentBench primary→demoted,
"primary eval" reassigned at least three times. v1 (Rust, `../legend-v1`) → v2
(the Rust spec, never built as written) → v3_minimal (the C oracle). Ten thousand
lines of C, journaling, embeddings, causal predicates, an audit verb, and a
maintenance mode — for one user's game notes. And the causal machinery, the
flagship of Round 2, is barely used: `modal` populated **5 times**,
`correlated_with` **0 times across 39 causal facts** (`alchamancer-trial.md:741-745`).
The project's own note admits the "never call a correlation a cause" invariant is
safe only because *the model never picks the option at all*. That's ceremony, not
capability.

### 5. The reflexive loop manufactures motivated conclusions. (SERIOUS)

Memory, docs, evals, verdicts, and testimony are written by Claude, reviewed by
Claude, for Claude, judged by Claude. The go/no-go criterion was set by the same
process that then rendered GO. The clearest tell is the conflictqa pivot: the
famous-entity test embarrassed the store (multi-hop 2/4, consumer override), so
the eval was redesigned around synthetic alien-name entities where the store wins
8/8 — and this is recorded as **"confound removed"** (`memory/MEMORY.md:70`) rather
than "we changed the test until it passed." Both framings are defensible in
isolation; that's exactly the problem. When the grader, the subject, and the
beneficiary are one system, "it's useful" is unfalsifiable, and the natural
gradient is to keep reframing until the number is green. Even the trial's honesty
rule ("don't credit the file-memory wins") is the system policing its own
scorekeeping — commendable, but not independent.

## The ONE thing that would change my mind (falsifiable bar)

Legend already wrote the test; it just won't run it. So:

> **Run the SimpleQA study's arm B (Legend store) vs arm D (naive top-k RAG over
> the *identical* corpus), token-budget-matched, on *real* (non-synthetic)
> entities, answered by a third-party model that is not Claude and not in the
> author's loop, graded blind, with the broken-flip rate reported alongside the
> fixed-flip rate.** (`docs/eval-simpleqa-distillation.md` §1 claim 3, §5 arm D,
> §7.)
>
> **If arm B beats arm D by a margin that survives the bootstrap CIs the doc
> already specifies — I'm wrong, and the graph is paying for itself.** Until then,

# Memo 3 — Sofia Almeida (DX / knowledge-representation)

I read the two docs, the full MCP instruction blob (`legend.c:9506-9553`), the
audit implementation (`legend.c:8966-9391`), the save-side mint paths, and the
whole trial findings log. I came in wanting to know one thing: when a real agent
sits in this tool for weeks, does the contract hold their hand into the pit of
success, or does it hand them a map of tripwires and wish them luck?

## Overall verdict

**The syntax is learnable. The semantics are a minefield — and the mine is always
the same mine.** Legend has cleanly solved the easy half of the contract and left
the hard half to willpower.

The evidence for "learnable" is real and I want to credit it: Round 3 ran 110
invocations with **zero rejections**, against 5 across all prior rounds combined
(trial doc, Round 3 close). The *shape* of a payload — the JSON, the verb names,
the required fields — the model gets right essentially always. That's a
well-designed surface.

But every rejection is a *loud* failure, and Legend's actual problem is its
*silent* failures. Look at what the audit verb even exists to find: `prose_name`,
`phantom_close`, `status_fact`, `orphan`, `bloat` (`legend.c:9004-9006`). Not one
of those is a rejection. Every one is a write that Legend **accepted, reported
success on, and quietly corrupted the store with.** The contract bounces the wrong
things and swallows the wrong things. That inversion is the whole story.

The single most damning line in the entire trial record isn't a metric — it's the
parenthetical at trial doc lines 176-177: the author, *while writing up the lesson
about paragraph-named phantom elements*, minted a paragraph-named phantom element
the same way. "The class catches careful writers." A rule that catches the person
writing the rule about the rule is not a discipline. It's a design defect wearing
a discipline's clothes.

## The two root causes everything reduces to

**Root cause 1 — Legend has no literal type. Every string becomes a node.**
`plan_ref` (`legend.c:4182-4205`) resolves a name and, if it doesn't find one,
*mints an element* — unconditionally, whenever `listable` is set. A `changes.to`
value goes through it with mint enabled (`legend.c:5242`). A fact object goes
through the same path. An attr value too. So there is no such thing as a scalar in
this store: `power: 14`, `status: shipped`, a rationale sentence — all of them
reify into named graph nodes. The instruction spends four separate clauses begging
the model not to do the one thing the type system makes automatic: *"`to` is a
SHORT canonical value that itself becomes an element -- never a prose note"*
(`legend.c:9518-9520`); *"a fact object -- and every attr value -- becomes an
element named by it, so never pass prose"* (`legend.c:9531-9532`). That's not
guidance. That's the API apologizing for itself.

Downstream symptoms: `prose_name`, `bloat`, `status_fact`, the entire
`changes.to`-reifies-prose papercut that *dominated Round 4* (4 of 5 changes minted
300-542-char prose names, trial doc Round 4 gauge 3).

**Root cause 2 — Legend cannot tell "refer to" from "introduce." An unknown name
is never an error; it is always a mint.** There's a `forced_new` flag to *assert*
newness (`legend.c:4189`), but no inverse flag to *assert existence*. So when the
model writes `changes.target`, `resolves.o`, or a `merge` operand naming something
it believes already exists, and it's slightly off, Legend doesn't say "no such
element, did you mean…" — it fabricates the element and returns success. The
instruction, again, does the API's job in prose: *"the `changes` target must
ALREADY EXIST or it silently mints a phantom while the real value stays stale"*
(`legend.c:9518-9519`); the `resolves` object *"must ALREADY EXIST … if your
resolves target shows up in the frame's writes.minted_elements, you created a
phantom"* (`legend.c:9526-9528`).

Read that last clause as a UX spec and it's absurd: *the way you find out you
failed is to notice the thing you didn't mean to create, in a sub-array of a
success response, and manually retract it.* The trial confirmed twice (findings 6
and 10) that the signal "went unread." Of course it did. Success responses are not
where anyone looks for failures.

Downstream symptoms: `phantom_close`, `orphan`, the `source`-without-facts phantom
(fixed defensively at `legend.c:5438-5447`), the `changes.from` mismatch phantom
(fixed at `legend.c:5224-5240`), the cross-store stub-minting hazard (testimony 8
/ `#645`). Every one of these is the same bug wearing a different hat, and each got
patched *individually* at the storage layer instead of at the root.

**Which rules are load-bearing vs papering over a design that fights the user?**

- **Genuinely load-bearing** (keep, they model something real):
  recall-before-save (dedup is inherently a lookup); `changes` preserves history
  where a new fact would just accrete; `resolves` closes via an edge, not by
  mutating a summary; "prefer few precise elements." These are sound
  knowledge-graph discipline and I'd teach them to a human.
- **Papering over the design:** "target must already exist or it mints a phantom,"
  "`to`/fact-object/attr-value must be short and never prose," "check
  `minted_elements` for your phantom." All three are the two root causes leaking
  into the user's lap. They're only "rules" because the API refuses to enforce
  them itself.

## Are the repeat papercuts user errors or design errors?

They are **design errors**, and the trial has already run the experiment that
proves it. The 400-char summary rule was in the instruction and the schema; Round
4 measured a **98% violation rate** on summaries written *during* the round (43 of
44), and closed the item as *"the write-side length instruction is dead"* (`#150`).
Then — and this is the part that made me put my coffee down — the fix that shipped
for the *dominant* Round-4 papercut (`changes.to` prose) was, per the diff of
commit `9d745ee`, **another instruction line and a schema-description string.** The
trial documents the instruction route failing and reaches for the instruction
route in the same breath.

## Q3 — Is the orientation packet the right primary read surface?

Yes *for what it is*, but there's a category error lurking in the question. **The
orientation packet is not the dedup surface, and it was never going to prevent
duplicates.** Reuse-the-canonical-name happens through the *focused*
recall-before-save (the tiered resolver + `near_matches`, `cli.md:182-186`). The
packet is the session-start "what matters now" read — a fixed ~16-item,
10-per-section, newest-first snapshot (`cli.md:38-57`). If anyone is leaning on the
packet to avoid minting a second "AP migration" element, they're using the wrong
tool; the discipline correctly points them at focused recall instead.

As an *orientation* surface it's well-built — the caps (`legend.c:7680-7710`) plus
the `omitted` counts are an honest design, and they took a 51KB packet that was
getting `head -c`-guillotined mid-`overview` down to a whole 13.3KB (trial doc,
RESOLVED 2026-07-18). Credit where due.

But at scale it has a structural problem the trial has already caught firing: it's
a **fixed window over a monotonically growing store**, and the cap is
**newest-first**. Round 4 measured it climbing ~2.3KB per +82 elements toward the
20KB valve (`#141`). Two consequences:

1. **Token cost is bounded; orientation *completeness* is not.** Newest-first
   means the packet's shelf life is measured in store-months, and old-but-load-
   bearing decisions silently fall below the fold.
2. This collides head-on with the trial's *strongest* finding — testimony 8 /
   `#89`: **the stale, cold facts are the valuable ones** (a stale intent-vs-code
   gap *was* the bug). Newest-first caps are, by construction, most likely to hide
   exactly the aged decisions that finding says are worth the most. The store
   correctly refuses to prune them (good — `#89`), but the primary read surface
   hides them anyway. The packet doesn't need pruning; it needs *salience/
   importance* ranking, not recency, for the protected sections.

## Q4 — Is a "lint for memory" the right idea, or is it treating symptoms?

Both, and the proportion matters. A read-only, deterministic, human-adjudicated
scan is genuinely the *right* shape for the two checks that are real judgment calls
(`stale_open`, `near_dup`) — you cannot and should not automate "is this
abandoned" or "are these the same concept," and the refusal-to-auto-prune stance
(`cli.md:69-74`) is correct and well-argued.

But **5 of the 7 checks are symptom-trackers for a write API that can't say no.**
- `prose_name`, `bloat`, `status_fact` → root cause 1 (no literal type).
- `phantom_close`, `orphan` → root cause 2 (mint-on-unknown-reference).

If those two distinctions existed in the model, five of the seven checks would have
*nothing to count* — the illegal states would be unrepresentable, not detectable-
after-the-fact. `near_dup` has found **0 confirmed defects across 815 elements**
(`cli.md:117-119`), so it's earning almost nothing today. Which leaves
`stale_open` as the one check that's a lint on purpose rather than a lint by
necessity. A lint that mostly re-discovers what the writer couldn't help doing is a
smell. It's the memory equivalent of shipping a linter rule for "you used `==`
instead of `===`" instead of just using a language where `==` doesn't exist.

## Q5 / The single highest-leverage change

**Make it impossible for a bare string to silently become a node.** Split it two
ways, both cheap because the ingredients already exist:

1. **Value positions store literals, not elements.** `changes.to`, scalar fact
   objects, attr values — accept a string and store it *on the relation*, reifying
   into a node only when the payload explicitly asks (an element ref or
   `{ref: …}`). Reification becomes opt-in. This deletes `prose_name`,
   `bloat`-as-node, `status_fact`-as-node, and the entire Round-4 `changes.to`
   papercut in one structural stroke.
2. **Reference positions must resolve or error.** `changes.target`, `resolves.o`,
   `merge` operands take a *reference* that must exist; on a miss, **fail loudly
   and put the near-miss candidates in the error body.** The resolver *already
   computes* those candidates (`near_matches`, `legend.c:5547`) and the
   `forced_new` flag already proves the plumbing for an existence assertion exists
   (`legend.c:4189`) — the inverse "must_exist" is simply missing. Minting stays
   possible only through the explicit `elements[]` path.

Why this is the highest-leverage change and not better instructions: **the trial
has definitively shown the model respects hard errors (zero rejections) and
ignores soft signals (the instruction route is "dead," `minted_elements` "went
unread").** So the entire game is converting silent-accept-and-corrupt into
loud-reject-and-teach. It's a small diff (an existence check plus reusing
candidates you already compute) with a store-wide blast radius, and it takes
*nothing* away from the stale-intent value in `#89`.

(Honorable mention, the third redesign: collapse `facts` and `changes` so a second
assertion of the same single-valued (s,p) supersedes by default — the store knows
single- vs multi-valued from the template `expects`, so the model shouldn't have to
pick the verb at all. That's the clean answer to the "should `changes` recognize a
plain-fact prior" question the trial deferred at trial doc lines 566-573, and it
retires `status_fact` for good.)

## Bottom line

Legend's contract is a genuinely good knowledge-graph discipline strapped to a
write API whose default reflex is to say *yes* to everything and fabricate
whatever's missing. The discipline is long not because memory is hard but because
the API has offloaded its invariants onto the prose — and the trial has now run
three-plus rounds proving that prose can't hold them. The tell is structural: the
store rejects malformed JSON loudly and accepts malformed *meaning* silently, so
the model learns the syntax perfectly and never learns the semantics at all. The
audit verb is a well-built confession booth for exactly this.

The fix is not more instruction and not a smarter lint. It's making the API able
to say *no* — literals in value positions, hard errors (with candidates) in
reference positions. Do that and five of the seven audit checks lose their reason
to exist.

Key files: instruction blob `legend.c:9506-9553`; mint-on-unknown path `plan_ref`
`legend.c:4182-4205`; `changes` apply path `legend.c:5205-5279`; the two defensive
phantom patches at `legend.c:5224-5240` and `5438-5447`; audit scan
`legend.c:9268-9391`; packet caps `legend.c:7680-7710`; recency-vs-importance
tension between `cli.md:38-57` and trial doc `#89`.

# Memo 4 — Dr. Elena Vásquez (cognitive neuroscience / memory science)

## Opening read

I came in braced for the usual sin of computational "memory" projects: brain-region
cosplay bolted onto a database to borrow the prestige of neuroscience. Legend is
not that — or not mostly that. The team has done real reading (Hasher & Zacks,
Dasgupta's count-sketch, Lisman–Grace, Pearl, Spelke) and, more unusually, they
discipline their own metaphors in writing. `new_foundation.md:313` states outright
that the brain regions "own no state" and are "functions of the substrate, not
modules"; `:2836` flags the conviction axis as having "no direct neuromodulator";
the Spelke note (`project_native_core_competencies.md:12`) explicitly *rejects*
importing Spelke's perceptual content as a category error. That self-auditing is
rarer than it should be, and it is itself a scientific virtue.

But the honesty is uneven. In several load-bearing places the neuroscience has
been let go slack, and — more importantly — the single most scientifically
interesting phenomenon in the whole system (retrieval as reconstruction, and its
distortions) has been engineered *out* of the boundary and *out* of the
evaluation.

Two framing corrections. First, I evaluated the *shipping* system — the C oracle
(`legend.c`, ~10.3k lines; `embed.c`, 658 lines), whose status lattice
(`legend.c:2101`) and causal vocabulary (`legend.c:2113`) I confirmed directly —
not the historical Rust brain-region modules. Second, most of what Legend does
that *works* is, at the mechanism level, a bitemporal, deduplicated,
provenance-tracked knowledge graph with vector retrieval. The question I was asked
is whether the *memory science* is load-bearing on top of that.

## The strongest things that are RIGHT

**1. The reconstructive-memory core is correct and genuinely load-bearing.**
`new_foundation.md:316` — "the brain doesn't store events; it stores distilled
abstractions… Reconstruction rebuilds context from cues, not from retrieved audit
records" — is the correct reading of a century of work (Bartlett 1932; schema
theory; Fuzzy-Trace gist/verbatim). And it *drives an architectural commitment*:
no per-input record, raw text discarded, WAL dev-only (`new_foundation.md:319-322`).
When an analogy changes what the code stores, it has earned its keep.

**2. The gist/verbatim boundary is operationalized precisely — and testably.** The
"in-scope iff the substrate kept distinct facts, out-of-scope is what compression
destroyed" line (`project_counting_out_of_scope.md:16-31`) is the sharpest piece of
cognitive engineering in the project. Averaging N distinct people's ages is
in-scope (each a distinct gist trace); mention-counting is out (surface frequency
is verbatim, and verbatim decays). That is *exactly* the FTT gist/verbatim
dissociation, and the `support_count`/`access_count`-as-approximate-frequency
mapping (`project_brain_mirrors_compression.md:31-39`) is well-grounded in Hasher &
Zacks and the count-sketch literature.

**3. Prediction-error-gated supersession is their best biological hit — and
they're under-selling it.** Step 2 sets `supersession_threshold = base * (1 -
prediction_error)` (`new_foundation_v0_core.md:605`), with `prediction_error`
mapped to the dopaminergic novelty signal (`new_foundation.md:2843`, citing Lisman
& Grace 2005). A *surprising contradiction* opens belief-revision while a bland
restatement does not. That is the core empirical finding of reconsolidation
research — updating requires a **prediction error / mismatch at reactivation**
(Sevenster, Beckers & Kindt 2013). They arrived at the reconsolidation gating rule
without citing reconsolidation. They should.

**4. Arousal → salience → decay-protection is correct, specific neuroscience.** The
salience scorer boosting exact-value facts, corrections, and high-arousal inputs
against decay (`new_foundation_v0_core.md:968`) maps cleanly to
noradrenergic/amygdalar modulation of consolidation (McGaugh 2004). The four-axis
intent vector (conviction / prediction-error=DA / arousal=NE / curiosity) is the
most defensible neuromodulatory mapping in the design.

**5. Where they let the analogy GO, they get it right.** Human reconsolidation is
*lossy*. Legend deliberately departs: supersession keeps the old trace immutable
and queryable (`legend.c:40`), and *never revives* a superseded prior
(`legend.c:2444`). The "stale intent" testimony
(`project_stale_intent_diagnostic.md:10-16`) — a fact that drifted out of sync
with the code, whose *disagreement* was the bug — is a real demonstration that
keeping the superseded version visible has diagnostic value the brain throws away.
Non-lossy versioning is *better* than biology for an epistemic tool.

## The strongest things that are WRONG or unsupported

**1. Pattern separation is attached to the wrong operation.** The auto-memory and
pipeline map "dentate gyrus / pattern separation" onto the dedup/coreference step.
But DG pattern separation *orthogonalizes similar inputs to prevent interference*
(Leutgeb 2007; Bakker 2008) — it pushes apart. Legend's dedup/coref *collapses
surface variants into one element* ("$1.2M" ≡ "$1,200,000"). That is the
**opposite** operation — CA3 pattern *completion* / auto-association. The construct
is present and correct; the name is on the wrong verb. Labels drive thinking, so
this is worth fixing.

**2. Calling the `derived_from` DAG "the substrate's structural causal model" is a
category slip.** `new_foundation.md:1450`. `derived_from` is a **provenance**
lineage — which *belief* was distilled from which *event*. An SCM (Pearl) is
structural equations over **variables** with intervention semantics. Provenance ≠
causation. It matters because the doc leans on it to claim do-calculus falls out
cheaply later (`:1450-1455`). It does not.

**3. "Never promote correlation to cause" guards the wrong threat.** The invariant
(`docs/causal.md:35`) forbids an *internal* operation nobody was going to perform.
Meanwhile the real epistemic risk is unguarded: the *ingesting* LLM stamps `caused`
on something merely correlated, and Legend faithfully stores it forever. Legend
does zero causal discovery, zero confounder adjustment. It is a faithful scribe of
*asserted* causality and inherits 100% of the asserter's causal illusions.

**4. The Spelke framing is doing PR the implementation doesn't cash.** Seven "core
competence" kernels proposed; scope-lock cut it to ~1.5 shipped
(`project_native_core_competencies.md:22`). What survived is value-aware
supersession and calendar grounding — a data-normalization routine and a date
parser. Meanwhile the *one* kernel genuinely non-redundant with a frontier LLM —
**deixis / indexical grounding** ("now"/"here"/"I" → tick context, which the LLM
*cannot* recover from text alone) — was flagged "the keystone" and then **parked**
(`:16,22`). "Grand framing, utilitarian residue" is fair.

**5. Some analogies are frank decoration.** `new_foundation.md:353` argues for
emergent-over-declared ontology because "neurons don't have a `NeuronKind::Concept`
tag." Non-sequitur: neurons lack symbolic tags because the brain is *distributed
connectionist* — which Legend explicitly is not. The emergent-ontology bet may be
right, but on the Wolfram computational-irreducibility argument (`:415`), not the
neuron analogy. Likewise "replay = consolidation" doesn't apply to a symbolic graph
(no superposition to interfere). What the region routing *does* match is
**schema-consolidation** (Tse et al. 2007) — and they cite neither Tse nor the
schema literature. More of the right neuroscience would strengthen this.

## The single most important scientific risk

**Legend faithfully stores gist, but the answer is reconstructed by a component it
does not own, does not measure, and has evidence overrides the store.** In human
memory the store and the reconstructor are the same co-adapted system. Legend
splits them: substrate = faithful gist store, consumer LLM = reconstructor whose
parametric schema can override the stored trace. They have *direct evidence*: the
conflictqa eval showed multi-hop recall capped at 2/4 *even with a correct store*,
because the LLM's famous-entity prior overrode the memory. **That is the
misinformation effect / schema-override — the single most scientifically important
phenomenon in the entire system — appearing in their own data.** And the response
was to engineer it *away* (pivot to synthetic entities) to get a clean lift number,
rather than to characterize it.

## A testable prediction to settle the load-bearing question

To distinguish "works because it mirrors memory" from "works for mundane
engineering reasons," run ablations where brain-memory makes a counterintuitive
prediction a KV store does not:

- **Schema-congruence retrieval advantage (Tse 2007).** Hold `support_count`
  fixed; compare recall@k for a fact routed into a *dense existing region* vs an
  equally-supported fact that *spawned a new region*. If congruent facts retrieve
  better at matched support, the region-as-schema story is load-bearing; if
  identical, region structure is just clustering.
- **Reconsolidation signature (Sevenster & Kindt 2013).** Ablate prediction-error
  gating vs always-overwrite. Measure both update accuracy on surprising
  corrections *and* false-update rate on non-contradictory restatements. If gating
  beats always-overwrite on the false-update axis, the mechanism is real.
- **Gist double-dissociation.** Show Legend fails exactly where human gist-memory
  fails (mention-counting, superseded running totals) and succeeds where humans
  succeed — *and* does not produce DRM-style schema-consistent false facts at the
  substrate level even when the consumer LLM would.

## Bottom line

**The science is roughly two-thirds load-bearing, one-third ornamental — and the
team mostly knows which is which, which is why I'd back them.** The load-bearing
third is real and rare: reconstructive/gist memory driving the no-raw-storage
commitment, prediction-error-gated revision, and arousal-modulated salience. The
ornamental third — the anatomical module names, the neuron-tag argument, "replay =
consolidation," and most of the Spelke dressing — is disclaimed often enough to be
mostly harmless, but it lets three genuine errors hide in respectable company:
pattern-separation-labeled-as-completion, provenance-dressed-as-an-SCM, and a
correlation-to-cause guarantee that guards the threat that never happens while
ignoring the one that does.

Blunt verdict: the biological analogy is a *generative* success and an
*evidentiary* liability. It generated a better design than a naive KG team would
have built — but the project now cites biology as if it validates the design, when
almost every shipped win is still explained by mundane engineering, and the one
place biology makes a hard, falsifiable prediction has not been tested against a
mundane baseline. Run those ablations. If null, keep the architecture and *demote
the neuroscience from mechanism to scaffolding* in the prose. And stop deleting the
misinformation effect out of your evals. It is not a confound. It is the
phenomenon.

design. The C oracle uses a hand-rolled length-prefixed little-endian binary format
(`legend.c:7856` `snapshot_serialize`). Grep for `lz4`/`LZ4` returns nothing. The
format is better for it — self-describing and self-validating.

## Architecture

**One 10k-line translation unit is the right call here.** Sectioned S1–S10
(foundations → model → parser → resolve → plan → apply/tick → frame → persistence
→ CLI), 304 small named functions, builds with a single `cc` invocation + libc +
libm — no build system to rot. Verb dispatch is a flat `strcmp` chain
(`legend.c:10185–10231`) — boring in the good way.

**State modeling is textbook data-oriented** and matches `C-STAR.md`:
- Flat growable arrays `Element[]` / `Relation[]` (`:2156`, `:2164`), addressed by
  `u32` handles, never pointers held across a `realloc` (the `xgrow` doubling arena
  at `:420`).
- Derived indices (`by_norm`, `dedup_slots`, `cur_slots`, `rels_by_elem`,
  `elem_kind`; the `Hypergraph` struct `:2233`) rebuilt from persisted arenas via
  `rebuild_indices` and **never serialized**. Persist the source of truth,
  recompute the acceleration structures. Correct.
- String interning arena (`StrArena`, `:556`) with open addressing so equality is a
  `u32` compare.

**The plan/apply split is the standout.** `plan_submission` (`:4887`) builds a
`Plan` against the *immutable* graph and mutates nothing; `apply_plan` (`:5850`)
commits it. Because planning touches nothing, the `setjmp`/`longjmp` error trap can
bail mid-plan and the store is untouched (`tick_save` comment, `:6230`). That's a
transaction boundary you got structurally instead of bolting on rollback.

**The hidden coupling, such as it is:** module-level mutable scratch buffers reused
across calls — `g_pend_real`, `g_rel_real`, `g_rel_promoted` (`:5865`, `:5951`,
`:5953`), `g_snap_buf`, `g_err_candidates`, and `static` locals
`Plan`/`Submission`/`WriteReport` inside `run_cli`. The `static` is deliberate
(they must survive a trapped `longjmp`), safe today because a process handles one
request at a time under a per-call `flock`. But it makes the entire write path
**non-reentrant** — precisely the thing that would corrupt silently the day someone
makes `mcp-serve` concurrent. A tripwire with no sign on it.

## Correctness & safety

**Parse path — hardened.** The JSON tokenizer (`:897–1158`) rejects leading zeros
(`:1000`), trailing content (`:1156`), unescaped control chars (`:915`), lone/bad
surrogates (`:955–967`); unescapes in place with a provable `wp ≤ rp` invariant;
validates UTF-8 at the door (`:908`); caps recursion depth at 128 (`:1044`,
`:1088`).

**Persistence reader — hardened, and the best code in the file.** `snapshot_load`
(`:8049`) bounds-checks every count and length against remaining bytes *before*
allocating (`:8130`, `:8146`, `:8185`), range-checks every stored handle during
rebuild (`:8158`, `:8202`, `:8210`), rejects non-finite floats (`:8018` + `:8024`),
rejects redirect chains (`:8177`), verifies the ontology contract (`:8238`),
rejects trailing bytes (`:8233`). Exactly how you write a reader that eats hostile
input without UB; the 3000-iteration corrupt-snapshot fuzz gate proves it holds.

**Write semantics — where the bugs live, and where they've been found before.** The
`plan_*` state machine (`:4051–5836`, ~40 functions) handling
changes/supersession/merge-fold/collision-collapse is the most intricate code in
the repo. The comments are the tell: `finding 3` (`:6129`), `pin 6`, `pin 23`.
Bugs have been caught here and each left a regression test. Memory safety across
all of it is validated by running the full corpus + fixtures under ASan/UBSan.

Error handling is a single `fail()` funnel (`:329`) with stable codes.

## Persistence & determinism

Format (`:7856`): magic + version + declared-file-length + clock + policy + stamps
+ string table + elements + relations, all fixed-width little-endian, doubles as
raw IEEE bits. The declared length must equal the file size (`:8099`). Write is
crash-safe: serialize to buffer → write tmp → `fsync(file)` → `rename` →
`fsync(dir)` (`:7919–7956`), plus an orphan-`.tmp` sweep under the lock on load
(`:7960`).

**Byte-for-byte replay is a real guarantee, within a binary version — and the gate
proves it.** `check.sh` replays the corpus twice and asserts *both* byte-identical
frame streams *and* byte-identical final snapshots (`:459`, `:464`). Holds because
(a) no `-ffast-math`, and (b) the only clock value that lands in the snapshot flows
through the single `LEGEND_NOW` seam (`apply_plan:5859` ← `now_unix_seconds:375`).

**What breaks it, honestly:**
1. A semantic fix that changes computed values *across* versions — acknowledged;
   the live snapshot is the source of truth, not cross-version byte-equality.
2. `journal.jsonl` itself is **not** byte-reproducible — its `ts` field uses raw
   `time(NULL)` (`:8364`, `:8899`, `:10075`), by design. "Determinism" means
   snapshot + frames, never the journal file.

## Embeddings (`embed.c`, `vectors.bin`)

Impressively self-contained: a full BERT encoder — token/pos/type embeddings, 12
layers of attention + FFN + LayerNorm + erf-GELU, CLS pooling, L2 normalize — in
~300 lines of libc + libm (`:233–310`), with an int8-dequant loader (`:316`), an
AVX2/FMA dot product gated at runtime by `__builtin_cpu_supports` with a scalar
fallback (`:167–209`). The `vectors.bin` sidecar keys each vector by
`(id, fnv64(text))` and stamps a `blob_sig = size ^ (mtime<<1)` that invalidates
the whole cache on a model swap (`:455`, `:477`). Graceful degradation everywhere:
no model dir → `embed_rank_elements` returns −1 → salience backfill
(`legend.c:3417`); `LEGEND_EMBED=0` disables cleanly; save-time sync uses a
stat-only gate to skip the 23MB load when nothing changed (`:558`).

**Failure modes / smells:**
1. **Identity drift — the one that actually worries me.** The code *is*
   BGE-small-en-v1.5 and computes it correctly: `NL=12` (`:32`), CLS pooling at row
   0 (`:302`), the BGE asymmetric query prefix (`:584`). But the file's own
   comments still describe the *old* model: `embed.h:1` "MiniLM", `embed.h:13`
   "mean-pooled", `embed.c:1` "all-MiniLM-L6-v2", `:12` "6-layer encoder →
   mean-pool", and `:31` labels the enum "fixed architecture (all-MiniLM-L6-v2)"
   directly above `NL = 12`. On-disk magic is `"MINILM02"` (`:366`) and the weight
   file is `minilm.int8.bin` inside `models/bge-small-en-v1.5/`. `docs/embeddings.md`
   is correct; only source comments and asset names lag. Nothing functionally
   wrong today — but in the single most reproducibility-sensitive component, the
   comments actively *lie* about what the code computes (mean-pool vs CLS-pool, 6
   vs 12 layers), and the misleading filename is a trap for the exact operation
   where getting it wrong corrupts everything silently: regenerating the weight
   blob.
2. **O(n²) cache access.** `ec_vec_for`/`ec_stale` linear-scan the sidecar per
   element (`:489`, `:549`) → quadratic in candidate count. At ~115 vectors it's
   microseconds; a scaling cliff, not a bug.
3. Sidecar never evicts dead ids — grows monotonically.
4. `wordpiece` is ASCII-complete only; Unicode/CJK are explicit TODOs (`:96`).

## Testing

Strong, and it gates the things that matter. `legend_test.c` is 56 test functions
/ 6799 runtime checks; `check.sh` layers 10 golden fixtures against *both* strict
and ASan binary, two corpus replays with byte-identical assertions, an MCP protocol
smoke, and payload+snapshot fuzzing.

**Coverage gaps:**
- **The embedder is the least-tested code in the repo.** `check.sh` runs almost
  everything with `LEGEND_EMBED=0` (`check.sh:31`); only the adversarial slice
  flips it on. The 300-line neural net, sidecar staleness/invalidation, and int8
  loader are barely exercised. `embed.c` ships its own `EMBED_TEST` golden harness
  (`:614`) — not wired into `check.sh`. It *should* be a gate step.
- **Concurrency** is one `flock`-conflict unit test. The warm `mcp-serve`
  reload-on-fingerprint path and real parallel-writer contention aren't
  stress-tested — the same path the non-reentrant global scratch would break.
- No canary distinguishing an intended cross-version snapshot change from an
  accidental one; today it's caught by eye.

## The single change I'd make first — and why

**Kill the embedder identity drift.** Rename the MiniLM references in
`embed.c`/`embed.h` to BGE (or add a one-line header disclaimer), fix the comments
that say "mean-pool" and "6-layer" to match the CLS-pool / 12-layer code, and — if
willing to touch the asset — rename `minilm.int8.bin` and the `"MINILM02"` magic.

Why first, over the more "serious" non-reentrancy debt: it is the only place where
the comments *contradict the code they sit on*, it lives in the most
determinism-sensitive component, and the wrong name is a live trap for the one
operation — regenerating weights — where a pooling or layer-count mistake produces
vectors that are silently, subtly wrong and pass every existing test because the
gate runs with embeddings off. An afternoon, zero behavior change.

The non-reentrant global scratch is the bigger *structural* debt, but I would
**not** refactor it now — it's correct under the current single-threaded-under-
flock contract. Instead: one comment at the top of `apply_plan` documenting
"single-threaded; `g_*` scratch is reused across calls — do not parallelize
`mcp-serve` without threading these through a context." Put a sign on the tripwire.

Minor housekeeping: `models/bge-small-en-v1.5/model.safetensors` (133MB source) is
tracked in git alongside the derived 33MB int8 blob — heavy for a "libc-only,
single `cc`" project. Consider a fetch script.

**What I ran:** `./check.sh` → `all green` (57s); strict compile of
`legend.c`+`embed.c` under `-Werror` → rc 0, zero warnings; confirmed no
LZ4/msgpack in the store path; confirmed the only snapshot-bound clock value flows
through the `LEGEND_NOW` seam.

# Memo 6 — Dr. Priya Nair (ML / LLM-memory research & eval rigor)

**Disposition:** One genuinely novel idea, a lot of competent reinvention, and an
evidentiary base currently too self-referential to support the headline claims.

A framing note up front: the elaborate hypergraph/activation-spreading/brain-region
machine in `new_foundation.md` is **not what shipped**. The `v3_minimal` pivot
threw most of it out. What exists on this branch is a single-file C oracle: a
deduped, revisable `{s,p,o}` graph over named elements, tiered focus resolution, a
section-capped orientation packet, and a deterministic audit. I evaluate that.

## 1. Novelty standing

**Where Legend sits.** Squarely in the "extraction-based structured memory" family
— the neighborhood of mem0, Zep/Graphiti (bi-temporal edge validity + automatic
invalidation), Microsoft GraphRAG (entity graph + community summaries, but
offline/corpus-oriented, not revisable at runtime), Letta/MemGPT (OS-style tiered
self-editing memory), LangMem. Decisively *not* long-context; a strict superset of
vanilla top-k RAG.

**Genuinely novel — one thing, clearly:** the **causal-rung typing of edges**
(`docs/causal.md:24-37`). Predicates partitioned by Pearl rung — `caused`/
`enables`/`prevents` (rung-2) vs `correlated_with` (rung-1) — with a hard invariant
that Legend *never* promotes `correlated_with` to `caused` (`:36`), plus a
per-claim `modal` array (`intervened`/`non_actual`/`negated`/`uncertain`/`general`,
`:42-61`). I am not aware of any deployed LLM-memory system that types its edges by
causal commitment and modality. Whether it is *useful* is unproven; the
representation is new.

**Novel as a design stance (not a research contribution):** the
**audit-as-suspicion, never auto-repair** philosophy (`docs/cli.md:63-89`) and the
"stale intent has diagnostic value" principle. Most systems aggressively
auto-consolidate; deliberately not doing so, and treating drift as information, is
a thoughtful contrarian call. The determinism story is the other real asset:
build-aware journaled replay reconstructing the store byte-for-byte across eight
binaries (`docs/alchamancer-trial.md:74`).

**Reinvention (say so plainly):**
- **Revisability + supersession with history.** `changes {target,property,to}` =
  latest-wins with the prior preserved (`docs/cli.md:157-171`). A cleaner,
  deterministic version of Zep/Graphiti valid-time invalidation and mem0's
  LLM-driven updates. Mechanism nicer; idea is prior art.
- **Bitemporal split** (tick = transaction time; valid time on meta-relations) is
  Zep/Graphiti's bi-temporal model.
- **Tiered focus resolution** (exact → alias → lexical → embedding,
  `docs/cli.md:183-186`) with asymmetric BGE retrieval is standard hybrid retrieval.
- **Dedup discipline** (recall-before-save) is what mem0/Zep do *internally*;
  Legend pushes it onto the calling LLM via prompt discipline — a defensible
  transparency choice, but architecturally offloading the hard part (entity
  resolution, extraction quality) to the consumer.

Net: **one novel primitive (causal-rung edges), one novel product stance
(non-destructive audit), strong reproducibility engineering, and an otherwise
well-executed recombination of mem0/Zep/GraphRAG ideas.**

## 2. Is the retrieval design well-founded? Where it will fail

Shipped recall: resolve focus terms through the tiers, return the focus subgraph +
a **1-hop** `related` band (RRF over activation and recency, `legend.c:6945-6974`)
+ typed sections capped at 10 each (`docs/cli.md:38-57`). Reasonable,
well-instrumented single-hop design. Failure surfaces, several documented in their
own baselines:

- **Cold / conversational recall is weak — measured, by them.** Ambient recalls
  "barely resolve (10/130, all lexical)"; the other 120 fall to candidate ranking
  where bookkeeping pollution dominated (rank-1-is-noise 78%,
  `docs/alchamancer-trial.md:456-464`). They fixed the noise *reordering*, but the
  underlying problem — short non-canonical focus text resolves poorly, and BGE
  cosines compress into a razor-thin 0.5–0.7 band with a 0.03 top1–top2 margin
  (`:470`) — is intrinsic and unfixed.
- **Multi-hop is not in the substrate.** No server-side path-finding; delegated to
  the *consumer LLM* chaining recall calls (focus=[work] → focus=[author] →
  focus=[spouse]). Works only when intermediate entities are unambiguous tokens;
  degrades sharply with name ambiguity. The graph is not doing the reasoning.
- **Contradiction resolution is a policy, not a resolution.** `changes` is
  latest-wins; it cannot weigh "source A vs source B, which is more reliable?"
  Correct for a single trusted author, wrong for multi-source ingestion.
- **The fixed packet doesn't scale cleanly.** Sections capped at 10 newest-first,
  but grew 12.7 → 15.0 KB, "climbing ~2.3 KB per +82 elements"
  (`docs/alchamancer-trial.md:842-843`). "Which 10 decisions surface at 900
  elements" answered with recency, not relevance.

Well-founded for a single-author, few-hundred-element store with an attentive
consumer. Unproven — by their own risk register — at 10k–50k facts
(`docs/eval-simpleqa-distillation.md:233-234`).

## 3. Eval rigor audit

### conflictqa — the "arm A 0/8 → arm B 8/8" headline

**What it tests** (`benchmarks/conflictqa/synth_run.py`, `run.py`, `prompts.py`,
`results/report_synth.md`): synthetic entities with no parametric prior; a store
built with 18 latest-wins conflicts; consumer answers with no store (A) vs a
`legend_recall` tool (B); exact-match; N=8 (4 single-hop + 4 multi-hop). Arm B gets
8/8 including 4/4 multi-hop.

**What it genuinely demonstrates, and would survive review:** latest-wins
**supersession works end-to-end** — the *post-conflict* value surfaces and gets
used, including the hardest case where the correction lands in a later chunk than
the original (`synth.py:127-137`). A legitimate correctness demonstration.

**Why it is not a memory-value result:**
1. **No baseline isolates the graph.** Arm A is "no store at all." For entities
   that exist *only* in the store, store-beats-nothing is near-tautological. No RAG
   arm and no "dump the 66 facts into context" arm — and with 66 facts (a few
   hundred tokens) the entire corpus fits in-context, where a strong model would
   also score ~8/8. So the graph *structure* buys nothing measurable here. Their
   own simpleqa design names this exact missing comparison ("Claim 3 — Structure,"
   `docs/eval-simpleqa-distillation.md:54`); conflictqa does not test it.
2. **The consumer prompt teaches the traversal.** `prompts.py:41-65` tells the
   model "the triple always wins," "copy the object string VERBATIM into your next
   recall," "never introduce a name that did not appear as the OBJECT of a triple."
   Arm B measures "store returns the right fact + model follows a spoon-fed
   algorithm."
3. **N=8.** No inferential weight.
4. **Provenance drift:** the project memory note describes this as a "deterministic
   $0 store load," but the committed `report_synth.md` is the **LLM-ingested**
   variant ($0.66, 3 saves, 18 conflict updates). Both code paths exist
   (`synth_run.py:124-140`); the headline number and the "$0 deterministic" framing
   come from different runs.

The famous-entity variant (arm A 1/8 → B 4/8, `results/report.md`) is honestly
reported as confounded — the frontier consumer overrides retrieved counterfactuals
with world knowledge. Pivoting to synthetic entities to kill that confound was the
right instinct; it just landed on a tautology instead.

### simpleqa — great design, essentially unrun

The **design** is what a skeptic would write: four arms including a **RAG arm D
with token-budget parity** (`:146-151`), explicit **broken-flip tracking** ("the
number nobody reports," `:189-193`), a three-bucket ingestion/retrieval/consumption
error triage, bootstrap CIs, question-blindness, a corpus-support audit, a
`LEGEND_EMBED=0` ablation, a contamination pilot-gate. If this ran at 500 questions
and cleared its gates, it would be publishable.

The **reality**: a 5-question smoke, 4 surviving audit, arm A 1/4 → B 2/4, one
fixed flip (`benchmarks/simpleqa/results/report.md`), and the `flips.md` grounding
worksheet has **unchecked boxes** — the hand-verification the README calls "the
point of Phase 0" (`benchmarks/simpleqa/README.md:49-51`) was never completed. So
there is currently **zero verified simpleqa signal**. And the smoke surfaced a real
quality red flag: 7 pages produced **1003 elements, 971 minted** (`report.md:29`) —
~140 elements/page, severe over-extraction.

### memoryagentbench — not a live eval

No independent harness. Two parquet files; `conflict_resolution.parquet` is
consumed by conflictqa as its "famous" corpus, `accurate_retrieval.parquet` (20 MB)
is unused. The "sh_6k 91/100" baseline in project memory is from the v1 Rust era.
On `v3_minimal`, MAB is a data dependency, not a benchmark that runs.

### replay corpus + check.sh — real, but a category error to call it a memory eval

The determinism byte-replay and adversarial probe slice are strong **regression/CI**
engineering — they verify the substrate does what it says and is byte-deterministic
across builds. Genuine hygiene, rated highly. But it compares against no baseline
and measures no downstream task, so it is not evidence that *memory helps*, only
that *the mechanism is correct*.

**Strongest result that survives peer review today:** "deterministic latest-wins
supersession surfaces the current value end-to-end, including cross-chunk
conflicts" (conflictqa) and "byte-identical journaled replay across eight binaries"
(trial). Both correctness/reproducibility results. **Nothing currently on disk
survives review as a memory-*value* result** — the one eval designed to produce it
(simpleqa §1-12) has not run.

## 4. The alchamancer2 trial

**What it establishes:** (a) the system runs for weeks without corruption; (b) it
is an excellent **failure-discovery instrument** — phantom mints, prose-name
pollution, predicate proliferation, over-extraction, status-fact staleness all
surfaced here; (c) qualitative existence proofs that a saved decision/recipe/rule
was occasionally reused across sessions.

**What it cannot establish: memory value.** N=1 user, the tool's **author**. The
subject and evaluator are **both Claude**, and the "evidence" is **self-authored
testimonies** saved as elements (#88/#89, `docs/alchamancer-trial.md:659-697`).
There is no control arm — no matched session run *without* Legend — so "attributable
wins vs cost" is adjudicated by the beneficiary. Introspective self-report, not
measurement.

**Is the pre-registration real?** Partly, and it is the most credible
methodological move in the project. The go/no-go criterion was locked 2026-07-13
*before* the countdown sessions (`:425`, `:498-505`), the Round-4 gauges written at
round-open, anchored to a baseline (`:816-831`), and they honestly report a
pre-registered **failure** (the 400-char gauge, 43/44 over budget). Reporting the
negative is the genuine article.

Three things blunt it: (1) the gauges measure **hygiene/mechanics** — packet size,
summary length, phantom counts — not task performance; you can pre-register a
thermometer and still not be measuring the disease. (2) The headline "GO-deepen"
verdict (`:509`) is *not* one of the pre-registered quantitative gauges; it is
rendered from six unblinded self-testimonies. (3) Round 4 **stopped at 7 sessions
against a pre-registered 15–18 target** and absorbed the shortfall as "findings
directional" (`:838`); Round 5 is explicitly "not a formal round."

## 5. The single biggest threat to the evidence

**The evaluator, the subject, and the author are the same system.** Every "win" is
self-reported by the agent that benefits, with no control condition; the only
quantitative lift (conflictqa) has no baseline separating the graph from "put the
facts in the context window"; and the one rigorously-designed eval (simpleqa) has
not produced a verified number. That is a structural circularity that makes the
"Legend helps" claim currently unfalsifiable from the inside. Motivated reasoning
is the *default* outcome of this setup, not a risk at the margin.

## 6. The one experiment that would move me

**Run the simpleqa §1-12 study you already designed — at 500 questions, with the
graph's contribution isolated by token-budget parity — and make the RAG comparison
the primary endpoint.** Keep arms A (bare), B (Legend), D (naive RAG at parity),
add a **long-context arm** (same retrieved-text token budget stuffed raw), run the
`LEGEND_EMBED=0` ablation, pre-register the **structure delta (B − D)** as the
headline with a bootstrap CI. Decision rule fixed in advance: if B beats D at token
parity with a CI excluding zero, the graph is paying for itself. If not, the honest
conclusion is "good extraction/dedup discipline, not a better substrate."

If the real target is *agentic* memory (the stated domain), the more decisive
version is a **multi-session, long-horizon agentic benchmark** (LongMemEval-style)
with a **matched, blinded cohort**: identical agent model, half the runs backed by
Legend, half by a strong RAG/mem0/Zep memory, graded blind on task success and
cross-session recall. simpleqa-at-parity is the cheaper first cut; the agentic
cohort study is the one that would settle it.

The encouraging part: **they already designed the right experiment.** The gap
between Legend's evidence and a real result is almost entirely execution, not
conception.

## Bottom line

There is **one real result** (deterministic, history-preserving supersession
works, and the store replays byte-identically across builds), **one genuinely novel
idea** (causal-rung + modal edge typing) whose usefulness is entirely unproven, and
**one genuinely good design stance** (non-destructive audit / stale-intent-as-
signal). Everything framed as evidence that *Legend helps an agent* is currently
either a tautological store-vs-nothing lift on eight synthetic questions, or
uncontrolled self-report from an N=1 trial where the author, subject, and grader
are the same system. A **promising direction sitting on a motivated-reasoning
evidentiary base** — not yet a demonstrated win over strong RAG. The fix is not
conceptual: run the token-parity RAG comparison they already spec'd, with an
outside grader, and let the structure delta fall where it falls.
